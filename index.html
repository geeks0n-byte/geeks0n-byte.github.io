What is Spaceblox?

Calm color logic in deep space.

Spaceblox is a calm color-logic puzzle set in soft pixel space. You fill an empty grid with yellow, blue and green tiles, and every color has its own rules: never three of the same color in a row, keep yellow and blue balanced on every line, and use green — which counts as both yellow and blue — at most once per line. Purple tiles that hop between linked cells, plus "=" and "×" links between cells, add fresh twists as the campaign moves from easy to medium to hard.

There is a guided tutorial, a full rule guide, hints when you are stuck, unlimited undo and redo, three optional star goals on every level, and achievements to chase (in the browser now, and on Android from the upcoming 1.1.0 update). The game is translated into seven languages: English, Spanish, German, French, Polish, Georgian and Ukrainian.

Design

Why Spaceblox feels calm

Spaceblox is built for five spare minutes or a whole quiet evening: clear rules, no pressure and no tricks. You should always be able to work out why a move is right — that "aha" moment is the whole point.

Rules first, luck never

Spaceblox puzzles are built so they can be solved by reasoning alone. The game highlights broken rules in red as you play, so you learn from mistakes immediately instead of discovering them at the end.

Calm by design

There is no game-over countdown and no energy system. A level never fails when time runs out; time only decides whether you earn the optional time star.

Respectful of players

Game progress is stored on your own device by default. Where consent is required, Google's certified consent tool asks before Google Analytics or AdSense use cookies on this website, and you can change your choices at any time from the "Privacy settings" link in the footer. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

Accessible where possible

The browser version of Spaceblox, and the upcoming 1.1.0 update for Android, include optional colorblind tile patterns and screen-reader labels for menus and the game screen, so the color rules do not rely on color alone.
