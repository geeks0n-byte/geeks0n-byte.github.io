Designing a calm puzzle game

By Giga Sichinava · 2 October 2026

When I describe Spaceblox, the word I use most is calm. That is not just about the soft pixel-space art. It shaped a lot of concrete design decisions. Here are the main ones.

Pressure is optional

Every campaign level has three stars: one for solving it, one for solving it without hints, and one for finishing within a time limit (or, on boards with purple tiles, within a limited number of hops). The important word is optional. The time limit only decides whether you earn that star: a level never fails because time runs out. If you want to sit with a hard board for twenty minutes, you can. If you want a challenge, replay it later and chase all three.

Mistakes should be cheap

A logic puzzle is frustrating when one wrong guess ruins ten minutes of work. Spaceblox tries to make mistakes cheap and informative:

  • a broken rule is highlighted in red immediately, on the exact cells involved;
  • undo and redo let you step back and forward freely;
  • press and hold clears a single tile, and restart returns the board to its starting layout;
  • your in-progress level is saved, so closing the game does not cost you anything.

Hints that teach

A hint that fills a cell for you solves the puzzle but teaches nothing. Spaceblox hints reveal a relationship instead — an "=" (same color) or "×" (different colors) between two cells. You still make the move yourself. Each level gives a few hints, more on harder difficulties, and you can choose to watch an ad for three more. Because the no-hint star exists, hints feel like a tool rather than a failure.

Teach one rule at a time

Spaceblox has several rules, and dumping them on the first screen would be overwhelming. The tutorial introduces them one by one, each on a small board with a highlighted cell to practise on. After that, the How to Play guide — with valid and invalid examples for every rule — is always one tap away, even mid-level.

Ads between moments, not during them

The games are free and supported by ads, so where ads appear matters. Full-screen ads are held back until a player has cleared several levels, and then they appear only between levels, never in the middle of a puzzle. Ads for extra hints are always the player's choice. On the website, a consent message lets you decide whether advertising and analytics cookies may be used, and you can change that choice at any time.

Don't rely on color alone

A color puzzle is a strange thing to make accessible, but it matters. Spaceblox has an optional colorblind mode that adds patterns to the tiles, with a palette adjusted for better separation, and screen-reader labels across the menus and game screen. Both are in the browser version now and arrive on Android with the upcoming 1.1.0 update. It is translated into seven languages, including Polish, Georgian and Ukrainian.

None of these ideas are new on their own. Together they are what I mean by a calm puzzle game: one where the only thing pushing back on you is the puzzle.
