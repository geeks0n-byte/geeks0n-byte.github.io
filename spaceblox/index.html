Spaceblox

I am Giga Sichinava. On Google Play my name is gix0n. Spaceblox is the puzzle I designed, programmed, and published. It is a color-logic grid, not a match-3. You fill empty cells with yellow, blue, and green tiles until every rule on the board holds. A few tiles are already placed. Some of those are locked. Later levels add purple tiles that hop, and "=" or "×" links between cells.

You can get it on Google Play or play the browser version. The Android app is free and contains ads. The browser version is free too. The Google Play launch on 1 September 2026 is v1.0, and that upload is the app only. The current public version is 1.1.0, dated 5 October 2026 on the press page. The browser version was released with 1.1.0. The changelog is the longer note on those two versions.

The game is built in Godot. The Android app and the browser build come from the same project. The browser version, from 1.1.0, is laid out in portrait, and it runs in a modern browser on a desktop or a phone.

What a level asks you to do

Each campaign level is a grid. Tap or click an empty cell to cycle Yellow, then Blue, then Green, and stop on the color you want. Press and hold a filled cell to clear it. In the browser, click and hold for about a second. Undo and Redo sit in the top bar, and you can use them as many times as you need. Restart puts the board back to the layout it started with.

If a placement breaks a rule, the cells involved turn red while you are still on the level. You do not wait until the end to find out. When every cell is filled and nothing is red, the level is solved, and you see how many stars you earned.

Purple tiles are already on the board when a level uses them. You do not cycle an empty cell into purple. Tap a purple tile and it hops along its arrow to the linked cell. If another purple tile is sitting on that destination, the hop is blocked until you move the one that is in the way.

There is a trailer on YouTube if you want to see a board before you play. The guide page has screenshots of the main menu, a puzzle in progress, the tutorial, level select, How to Play, achievements, the pause menu, and the victory screen.

Campaign and tutorial

The first time you press Play, Spaceblox offers a guided tutorial. It teaches one rule at a time on a small board, with a highlighted cell to practise on, and then moves to the next rule. You can replay the tutorial from the main menu whenever you want the rules again in that same order.

After the tutorial, the campaign runs through easy, medium, and hard levels. They unlock as you go. Early boards stay with yellow and blue. Later boards bring in green, then purple hoppers, locks, and links. I have not published a level count, so I will not guess one here. The in-game How to Play button opens the rule guide, with valid and invalid examples, at any time, including in the middle of a level.

The rules themselves are short. The Rule of Two says you may not place three tiles of the same color next to each other in a row or a column. Two yellows side by side are allowed. A third is not. Equal Balance says a finished row or column must contain the same number of yellow tiles and blue tiles. Green counts as both yellow and blue for the Rule of Two, and as neither for Equal Balance. A line may hold at most one green tile. That is the Rule of One. Purple fills a cell and, like green, does not add to the yellow or blue count. A lock icon means the tile is fixed. = means they must match and × means they must differ. The full walkthrough, with text grids, is on the how to play page.

Hints, undo, and stars

A hint does not fill a cell for you. It reveals a helpful "=" or "×" link on the board, and you still choose the colors. Each campaign level gives you a small number of hints. Harder levels give you more. If you use them up, you can watch an optional ad to receive three more. You never have to. Solving without a hint is one of the star goals, so a hint is a tool you spend, not a step the game forces.

Undo and Redo are not rationed. Hold-to-clear removes one tile. Restart restores the starting clues. Your in-progress level is saved, so closing the game does not throw away the board you were on.

Each campaign level has up to three star goals:

  1. Solved. Finish a valid board.
  2. No hints used. Finish without using a hint.
  3. Time, or hops. Finish within the level's time limit. On boards with purple tiles, this star can instead ask you to use no more than a set number of purple hops.

The clock never ends the level. If you miss the time, that star stays empty and the puzzle stays open. Stars are optional. You can clear a level with one star and come back later for the others. Achievements track milestones, star goals, and a few secret feats, with bronze, silver, and gold tiers, plus hidden ones, on the Achievements screen.

Colorblind mode, screen reader, and languages

Colorblind mode is optional. It adds patterns to the tiles and uses a palette adjusted for better separation, so a color is not the only way to tell tiles apart. Screen-reader labels cover the menus and the game screen. On Android, version 1.1.0 lists TalkBack support together with colorblind mode. The browser version released with that same update has the patterns and the labels too. v1.0, the Play app from 1 September 2026, does not.

The game text is in seven languages: English, Spanish, German, French, Polish, Georgian, and Ukrainian. If a phrase is wrong, the contact page is the place to send the language, the screen, and the corrected text.

Other settings are plain on purpose. You can choose a dynamic or a static space background, and music and sound effects are separate.

v1.0 and the current version, 1.1.0

v1.0, on 1 September 2026, is the campaign. Easy, medium, and hard levels, the tutorial, hints, undo, and the three star goals all shipped then. The color rules did not wait for a later update. Yellow, blue, green, purple hoppers, locks, and "=" / "×" links are the v1.0 puzzle. Seven languages shipped with it. So did on-device saves, and ads that stay between levels.

1.1.0, on 5 October 2026, is the current public version. On Android it adds four things the home page lists: achievements, cloud saves, colorblind mode, and TalkBack support. The same version is the first web release. Play stays first, on Google Play. The browser version is second. It has the campaign. It does not have cloud save. From 1.1.0 on Android you can sign in to Google Play Games if you want a cloud copy of progress, settings, and achievements. In-progress levels are not uploaded. Signing in is never required. The browser save stays in that browser.

The same Android update adds Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics. Earlier app versions, including v1.0, do not include Firebase. Analytics records events such as a level started, a level completed, or a hint used. Crashlytics sends a crash report. The privacy policy is the full description. None of that changes how a cell is filled.

If you installed the game on 1 September 2026 and you have not updated, you are on v1.0. That copy is Play only. It does not yet have cloud save, colorblind mode, or TalkBack, and it is not the web build. Update to 1.1.0 for those Android additions, or open the browser version that shipped with 1.1.0. The color rules are the same in v1.0 and in 1.1.0.

How it is paced, and how the ads work

I wanted a puzzle you can open for a few minutes or sit with for an evening. There is no energy meter and no game-over countdown. A level does not fail when time runs out. Time only decides an optional star. Every campaign puzzle is built so it can be solved by reasoning. If a board feels like a guess, the red cells are telling you which rule broke, and a hint will add a link instead of skipping the step.

Spaceblox is free. The Android app shows ads with Google AdMob. This website uses Google AdSense. Full-screen ads are held back until you have cleared several levels, and then they appear between levels, not in the middle of a puzzle. An ad that pays out extra hints is always your choice. Where consent is required, Google's certified consent tool asks before analytics or advertising cookies are used on the site. The privacy policy is the full account of what is stored and what is not.

Progress stays on your device unless you choose otherwise. You do not need an account to play. On Android, version 1.1.0 adds optional Google Play Games sign-in. If you sign in, Google stores a cloud copy of your progress, settings, and achievements. In-progress levels are not uploaded. The browser version saves in that browser on that device, and it does not have cloud save. Signing in is never required to play.

If something breaks, or a rule note is unclear, write to me from the contact page. I read every message.

Spaceblox changelog

This is the player-facing history of Spaceblox. I am Giga Sichinava (gix0n on Google Play). It covers two published versions. v1.0 is the Google Play launch on 1 September 2026. 1.1.0 is the current public version, dated 5 October 2026 on the live press page. The history stops there.

The Android app is on Google Play. v1.0 is that Play release only. With 1.1.0, Spaceblox was also released as a web version at /spaceblox/play/. The campaign, the tutorial, and the rules are in the app and, from 1.1.0, in the browser. Cloud save is Android-only. The browser build does not have it.

v1.0, 1 September 2026

v1.0 is the first release on Google Play. The live site describes that day as the first release: a color-logic campaign with easy, medium, and hard levels. The privacy policy uses the same date as its effective date.

What you do in v1.0 is fill a grid. Some cells already hold tiles. A lock icon means that tile is fixed. An empty cell cycles Yellow, then Blue, then Green. Purple tiles, when a level has them, are already on the board. You tap one to hop it along its arrow. If another purple tile is on the destination, the hop is blocked until you move the blocker.

The rules in that first release are the rules the game still teaches:

  • Rule of Two. No three tiles of the same color next to each other in a row or a column. Two of a color may touch. A third may not.
  • Equal Balance. A finished row or column has the same number of yellow tiles and blue tiles.
  • Green. For the Rule of Two, green counts as both yellow and blue. For Equal Balance, green counts as neither. It fills a cell and adds nothing to either count, which is what makes a line of 3, 5, or 7 cells possible to balance.
  • Rule of One. A row or a column holds at most one green.
  • Purple. A purple tile fills a cell and, like green, does not count toward Equal Balance. It hops between two linked cells.
  • Links. = means they must match and × means they must differ.

The campaign introduces those ideas gradually. Early boards stay with yellow and blue. Later boards add green, then purple hoppers, locks, and links. I have not published a level count, so this note does not include one. Levels unlock as you go, from easy to medium to hard.

Around the rules, v1.0 already had the tools the guide describes. The first time you press Play, a tutorial teaches one rule at a time on a small board, with a highlighted cell to try. You can replay that tutorial from the main menu. How to Play opens the rule guide, with valid and invalid examples, during a level. A broken rule highlights the cells involved, in red, while you are still playing. Undo and Redo are unlimited on a campaign level. Press and hold clears one tile. Restart restores the starting layout.

Each campaign level has up to three star goals. One is for finishing a valid board. One is for finishing without a hint. One is for finishing inside the level's time limit, or, on some purple boards, within a set number of hops. The clock does not end the level. Missing the time, or the hop limit, leaves that star empty. Stars are optional. You can clear a level with one star and come back for the others.

A hint does not fill a cell. It reveals a helpful "=" or "×" on the board, and you still choose the color. Each level includes a small number of hints, more on harder levels. If you run out, you can watch an optional ad for three more. You do not have to.

Progress in v1.0 stays on the device. There is no browser build in this version, and there is no account. I do not run a save server. An in-progress level is saved, so closing the game does not throw the board away. The game text is in English, Spanish, German, French, Polish, Georgian, and Ukrainian. Music and sound effects are separate. The space background can be dynamic or static.

The game is free. v1.0 is supported by ads: AdMob in the Android app, AdSense on this website. Full-screen ads are held until you have cleared several levels, and then they appear between levels, not in the middle of a puzzle. The privacy policy's summary for the app before 1.1.0 does not include Firebase. That arrives in the next version. v1.0 on Google Play is the campaign release. Achievements, cloud save, colorblind mode, and TalkBack are not in that upload.

1.1.0, 5 October 2026

1.1.0 is the current public version. The home page dates it 5 October 2026. The press page says the same. On Google Play the what's-new lines are:

  • Achievements
  • Cloud saves
  • Colorblind mode
  • TalkBack support

With 1.1.0, Spaceblox was also released as a web version. The Android app stays first: Google Play. The browser version is second: /spaceblox/play/. It is the same Godot project, laid out in portrait, and it runs in a modern browser on a desktop or a phone. Click a cell to cycle Yellow, then Blue, then Green. Click and hold for about a second to clear a tile. Progress stays in that browser on that device. There is no cloud save on the web, and browser progress does not move to the phone. v1.0 had no web build. This 5 October 2026 release is the first one.

Achievements. Bronze, silver, and gold tiers, plus hidden achievements. They reward milestones, star goals, and a few secret feats. You look them up on the Achievements screen. If you sign in to Google Play Games, achievements can sync. Signing in is optional, and it is never required to play.

Cloud save. Optional Google Play Games sign-in stores a cloud copy of your progress, settings, and achievements in your Play Games account, on Google's side. I do not keep that copy. In-progress levels are not uploaded. Browser progress stays in that browser on that device, and it does not move to the phone by itself. You can delete the on-device save from the system settings, by uninstalling, or with Reset profile in Options. You can delete the browser save by clearing site data for geeks0n-byte.github.io.

Colorblind mode. Optional. It adds patterns to the tiles and uses a palette adjusted for better separation, so a rule does not depend on hue alone. The setting sits with language, sound, and background. It is part of Android 1.1.0, and part of the browser version released with 1.1.0. I have not published the pattern shapes, so this note does not draw them.

Screen reader. Labels cover the menus and the game screen. The 1.1.0 Android line names TalkBack, Android's screen reader. The browser version released with 1.1.0 has those labels too.

Firebase, from 1.1.0 onward, Android only. The privacy policy says this version adds Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics. Analytics records gameplay events such as a level started, a level completed, or a hint used, plus basic device and app information. Crashlytics collects crash reports: the device model, the operating system version, and the state of the app when it crashed. Earlier versions of the app do not include Firebase. I use those reports to fix bugs. They are not a switch inside a level.

After you clear campaign levels, the app may show Google Play's in-app review prompt. Any rating you leave is handled by Google Play. This history does not quote ratings, and it does not include a count of them.

The published Android notes for 1.1.0 are those four home-page lines, plus Firebase in the privacy policy, plus the web release at the play page. The color rules did not change between v1.0 and 1.1.0. The Rule of Two, Equal Balance, green, purple hoppers, locks, and "=" / "×" links are the same rules the tutorial teaches. = means they must match and × means they must differ.

Where to play the current version

Install 1.1.0 from Google Play, or open the browser version that shipped with that same version. Both play the campaign. If you want cloud save or TalkBack on Android, you want 1.1.0, not the v1.0 upload from 1 September 2026. The browser build is the way to play without installing, and its progress stays on that device.
